## D-585 · A new end-to-end job is timed from its siblings, and the group target is 400 s
**Date:** 2026-11-18 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** amends D-307 (the groups) and D-484 (the target, 360 s) · **Topics:** tests
**Context:** `groups.test.mjs` failed on three PRs in two days (signature `14fef58185`: "the heaviest group goes far over the target"), each one adding a game. Every new job counted as a flat 90 s until measured, so a new game's four jobs (a conformance part takes ~11 s) added ~360 s on paper, and the session timed them by hand to get past it. Behind that, main itself sat at the edge: the 18 groups were 412–413 s against an alarm at 360 × 1.15 = 414 s, so any new game, even timed right, would turn it red. The groups are capped at 18 runners, and the suite (~7,400 s of browser time) has needed all 18 since D-484, so the target no longer chose the groups: at 360 or 400 s they are the same.
**Decision:** An unmeasured job counts as the median of its measured siblings: the other parts of the same script (`conformance.mjs:<game>`), else the same file in the other games' folders (`<game>/languages.mjs`), else 90 s (`estimate` in `tools/e2e/groups.mjs`; `ci.mjs` groups with it too). `TARGET_S` goes from 360 to 400 s, as D-421, D-447 and D-484 did before: the alarm stays (the heaviest group may not pass 1.15 × the target, now 460 s) and the test prints the headroom left before it.
**Why:** A red check a game PR cannot fix, whose fix is timing a script by hand, is chased again on every new game. The estimate is what the job will most likely take, so the groups and the alarm see the suite as it will be. The target names what the check really takes (~7 min plus setup); raising it changes no group and no test, and leaves room for about six new games before the next deliberate choice.
**Consequences:** `groups.mjs measure` still replaces each estimate with the real time. When the headroom the test prints runs low, the costs round (D-558) cuts browser time or proposes the next target, before a game PR hits the alarm.
